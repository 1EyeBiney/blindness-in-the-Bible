## 2 October 2026

**Asked:** When did "we walk by faith, not by sight" first become your
verse, and what was happening in your life at the time?

**Brian:** I have slowly come to having this as my verse because it took a
while to learn to walk by faith. This is true for those transitioning to
non-visual means in life as well. It takes some time to learn to trust new
methods and tools, like a white cane or a screen reader.

**Earlier the same day, on why he wants this project:** I am blind and a
Christian. I do think that God has a deep care for the blind. I truly
believe that we "walk by faith and not by sight", so that saying has a lot
of meaning for me.

**Asked:** Of the blind people in Scripture who were never healed (Isaac,
Jacob, Eli, Ahijah, Samson, Zedekiah), is there one whose story speaks to
you most?

**Brian:** I don't know that any blind characters in the Bible really speak
to me like that. I did not lose my vision to old age and was born with good
eyesight.

*Note for the studies:* this is itself worth writing about. Brian was born
sighted and lost his sight later, not in old age (he has not said when or
how). Scripture's blind people are mostly born blind, blind in old age, or
blinded by enemies or by God for a time. Two partial parallels to sight lost
after having it: Bartimaeus, who asks to "see again" (Mark 10:51), and Saul
on the Damascus road, whose sight returned after three days.

## 3 October 2026

**Reading John 9, on Jesus's answer that neither the man's sins nor his
parents' sins caused his blindness, but that the works of God might be
displayed:** This is very true for most any blind person. No matter how much
technology evolves, we are dependent on God and His presence through the
works of others, knowingly and unknowingly.

**On how the studies should feel:** My current pastor, John, puts us into the
places and times of whatever we are studying. I'd like to incorporate that
kind of thing here, even if it is secular in nature. I do not think it will
go against anything we are finding out, only make it more interesting.

## 3 October 2026, Brian's thoughts on the stories (his file, verbatim)

one of my first true walk by faith moments happened during my rehab training at the Hine VA Hospital just outside Chicago. At the end of my training, I did a "drop off" test where I was let out of a car in a business district of a suburb with the task of independently finding a grocery store about 5 blocks away. This was my first really solo experience in that I knew that unless I got into serious physical danger, I was not to be assisted. I had to trust my new skills and abilities to make that trip without any sight. It was terrifying, exhilarating and liberating all at the same time. This one makes me cry because of what Jesus first says:

back to the story: the man was blind from birth, and never had it before. From a physiological standpoint, his visual cortex would have never developed. It takes roughly 6 years for an average person to develop their visual cortex with enough stimuli to be able to distinguish human faces. This man travelled through the city from north to south and washed the mud off his eyes and he "received my sight" so this was new to him. Jesus not only headed the physical ailment, he must have given the man a developed visual cortex in which to process those images. We have evidence of people either regaining sight or getting it for the first time and they cannot process those images, See Mike May and his inability to render faces now and still uses Braille and mobility aids.
People did not recognize him likely due to the sheer enormity of what happened. If I walked up to someone now who has never known me with sight and all of a sudden I had it, I would talk different, carry myself different. I would still turn my head toward the sound, that's natural, but my approach and disposition would be different and I would react to visual ques that I do not react to now.
