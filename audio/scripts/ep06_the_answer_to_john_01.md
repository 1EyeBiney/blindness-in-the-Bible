# Not By Sight, episode 6: The answer to John

Script for reading. Speaker names in bold, cues in italics, Scripture indented.

**Narrator** *(calm)* A man is sitting in the dark, listening.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(beat)* Imagine the cell with him. It is cut out of solid rock, and rock keeps every sound. Water is dripping somewhere, slowly, always in the same place. When he shifts, the straw under him rustles, and the sound comes straight back off the walls. The air is cool and still, and it smells of damp stone.

**Narrator** *(calm)* From far above, through all that rock, comes something else. Music. Faint, like something half remembered. There is a palace up there, and people in it who have had plenty to eat.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(beat)* Then footsteps in the passage outside. A guard, in no hurry. They come closer, pass the door, and go on. He knows that walk by now. He was a man of the open air, who preached in the wilderness, beside a river, to crowds. Now the world reaches him the way it reaches a blind man: by sound, and by what other people tell him.

**Narrator** *(calm)* So there is one sound he listens for above all the rest. A key in the lock. A bolt drawn back. A door so heavy that it groans. That sound means a friend, let in for an hour, with news.

**Narrator** *(calm)* And it is the news that troubles him. He has been hearing things, secondhand, about a man he once pointed out to his own followers. What he hears does not sound like what he expected. He cannot go and see for himself. So he sends two friends, on a walk of four days, with one question.

**Narrator** *(calm)* The answer he gets back ... begins with blind people.

*(pause 1.5 s)*

**Narrator** *(calm)* This is Not By Sight, a series on blindness in the Bible, made by a blind Christian for blind believers, their families, and the church. The Scripture you will hear is the Berean Standard Bible, read word for word. The thoughts marked as Brian's are Brian Clark's own. And when we imagine a scene, we will tell you so.

**Narrator** *(calm)* This is the last episode in our run through the healings in the Gospels, and it is the one where Jesus Himself tells us what the healings were for.

---

**Narrator** *(calm)* First, where we are. John the Baptist baptised Jesus in the Jordan and pointed Him out to his own followers. Then John rebuked Herod Antipas for taking his brother's wife, and Herod put him in prison. Josephus, the Jewish historian, names the prison: Machaerus, a fortress east of the Dead Sea, on the frontier. Edersheim describes it as a stronghold on sheer cliffs, built by one Jewish king and enlarged by Herod the Great, with a palace at the top and dungeons cut into the rock below. John the Baptist would die there.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Where this is. Machaerus stands on the far side of the Dead Sea from everything else in this series, and it is the farthest south we go. Stand on its walls and face north, with north at twelve o'clock. Jericho is at eleven o'clock and Jerusalem at ten, and although both are close as the bird flies, the Dead Sea lies in between, so a messenger had to walk round its northern end: about two days to Jerusalem by way of Jericho. Straight ahead at twelve o'clock, up the whole length of the Jordan, is Galilee, where Jesus was. Nazareth, Capernaum and Bethsaida are all about four days' walk. That is how far John's two friends had to go to ask one question, and how far they had to come back with the answer.

**Narrator** *(calm)* From that place he sends his question. Luke tells it with the most detail.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Luke chapter 7, verses 18 to 23.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Then John’s disciples informed him about all these things.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* So John called two of his disciples and sent them to ask the Lord, “Are You the One who was to come, or should we look for someone else?”

> **Reader** *(plainly)* When the men came to Jesus, they said, “John the Baptist sent us to ask, ‘Are You the One who was to come, or should we look for someone else?’”

> **Reader** *(plainly)* At that very hour Jesus healed many people of their diseases, afflictions, and evil spirits, and He gave sight to many who were blind.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* So He replied, “Go back and report to John what you have seen and heard: The blind receive sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the good news is preached to the poor.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Blessed is the one who does not fall away on account of Me.”

**Narrator** *(calm)* Are You the One who was to come, or should we look for someone else? That is the whole question. And it comes from the last man you would expect to ask it. Why would John, of all people, send it? Matthew Henry offers three possible reasons, and they are worth taking one at a time.

**Narrator** *(calm)* The first is the plainest. John wanted to be sure, for himself. He had given his whole life to announcing this man, and a prisoner has a great deal of time to think.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(calm)* The second is harder to hear. John had been in that cell a long time. The One he had announced was free, and healing strangers, and had not come for him. Henry suggests that the long imprisonment had made John wonder why Jesus did not help him.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(calm)* The third is the kindest to John. Perhaps he was not asking for himself at all. He knew he might never leave that place. So he sent his followers to see Jesus with their own eyes, so that when he was gone, they would know whom to follow.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(calm)* We are not told which it was. And a person can ask one question for more than one reason.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Whatever the reason, Luke tells us what Jesus did before He answered a word. At that very hour He healed many, and gave sight to many who were blind. Henry takes the words literally: the messengers stayed perhaps an hour, and in that hour saw enough. Then He told them what to say.

---

**Narrator** *(calm)* To hear the answer the way John heard it, you have to know what John knew. Jesus's reply is built out of the prophet Isaiah. Here is the first passage, from a chapter about the day God Himself would come.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Isaiah chapter 35, verses 4 to 6.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Say to those with anxious hearts: “Be strong, do not fear! Behold, your God will come with vengeance. With divine retribution He will come to save you.”

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Then the eyes of the blind will be opened and the ears of the deaf unstopped.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Then the lame will leap like a deer and the mute tongue will shout for joy. For waters will gush forth in the wilderness, and streams in the desert.

**Narrator** *(calm)* And the second, the passage Jesus had already read aloud in the synagogue at Nazareth.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Isaiah chapter 61, verse 1.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* The Spirit of the Lord GOD is on Me, because the LORD has anointed Me to preach good news to the poor. He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives and freedom to the prisoners,

**Narrator** *(calm)* The eyes of the blind will be opened. Good news to the poor. Jesus did not say, I am the one. He said, go and tell John what you see, and then He listed the things Isaiah had said would happen when God came. John, who had Isaiah by heart, would have needed no explanation. Smith's dictionary says opening the eyes of the blind was understood as a peculiar attribute of the promised one. Henry says the works spoke more plainly than any words could.

---

**Narrator** *(calm)* Scripture gives us the question and the answer. It does not give us the messengers' return. We imagine two tired men climbing back up to Machaerus with something to tell. This is a scene, not Scripture.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Disciple** *(calm)* Teacher. We are back. Yes, it is us, both of us. Sit, please, you should sit.

**John** *(calm)* Well? Did you ask him? What did he say?

**Disciple** *(calm)* We asked him exactly as you said. Are you the one who was to come, or do we wait for another.

**John** *(curious)* And his answer?

**Disciple** *(calm)* At first, nothing. He went on with what he was doing. There was a crowd, as there always is, and people were being brought to him. We stood there an hour. A man who had been carried in walked out. A woman who was bent double stood up straight. And there was a blind man.

**John** *(calm)* I see. ... Go on.

**Disciple** *(excited)* He was old, and someone had him by the arm, and Jesus touched his face, and the man looked up, and he started to cry and to laugh at the same time, and he kept looking at his own hands. There were so many! More than one. So many. We lost count.

**John** *(whisper)* The eyes of the blind will be opened.

**Disciple** *(calm)* Then he turned to us and said, go back and tell John what you have seen and heard. The blind see. The lame walk. Lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor are told the good news. And then he said one more thing, and I think it was for you.

**John** *(calm)* Say it.

**Disciple** *(calm)* Blessed is the one who does not fall away on account of me.

*(pause 2.0 s)*

**Narrator** *(calm)* John stayed in Machaerus. He was not healed, and he was not rescued. The answer he got was wonders and signs for others.

---

**Narrator** *(dryly)* And now, a word from Brian. Or rather, from the voice Brian picked for himself, since he would not use his own. Around here we call it Mister Scaredy Cat. The voice is not his, but the words are.

*(pause 1.0 s)*

**Brian** *(calm)* John the Baptist doubted. The man who pointed at Jesus and said, behold the Lamb of God, sat in a dungeon and sent people to ask, are you the one, or should we wait for somebody else. For some reason, I find that enormously comforting. I think it means that having doubt while in a dark place is not a failure of faith. I think it is what faith sounds like when it is being tested, and it is allowed to ask.

**Brian** *(calm)* Jesus did not answer with a sermon, or an argument, or a proclamation. He answered with people. Luke says that at that very hour He healed many, and gave sight to many who were blind, and then told John's messengers, go tell him what you have seen. When the question was, who are you, the answer began with blind people seeing. I have heard that verse so many times and it still stops me.

---

**Narrator** *(calm)* Matthew tells the same exchange more briefly, and we will hear it, for two reasons. Matthew says John heard in prison about the works of Christ. It was the works that reached him, through the walls. And Matthew goes straight on to what Jesus said next, once John's friends had started for home.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Matthew chapter 11, verses 2 to 11.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Meanwhile John heard in prison about the works of Christ, and he sent his disciples

> **Reader** *(plainly)* to ask Him, “Are You the One who was to come, or should we look for someone else?”

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Jesus replied, “Go back and report to John what you hear and see:

> **Reader** *(plainly)* The blind receive sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the good news is preached to the poor.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Blessed is the one who does not fall away on account of Me.”

> **Reader** *(plainly)* As John’s disciples were leaving, Jesus began to speak to the crowds about John: “What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed swaying in the wind?

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Otherwise, what did you go out to see? A man dressed in fine clothes? Look, those who wear fine clothing are found in kings’ palaces.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* What then did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet.

> **Reader** *(plainly)* This is the one about whom it is written: ‘Behold, I will send My messenger ahead of You, who will prepare Your way before You.’

> **Reader** *(plainly)* Truly I tell you, among those born of women there has risen no one greater than John the Baptist. Yet even the least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.

**Narrator** *(calm)* Did you hear when He said it? As John's disciples were leaving. Jesus waited until they were on the road, and then He turned to the crowd and spoke about the man in the cell. Not a reed swaying in the wind. Not a man in fine clothes. Those, He says, are found in kings' palaces. John was underneath one. A prophet, and more than a prophet. Among those born of women there has risen no one greater.

**Narrator** *(calm)* So the doubt did not lower John in His eyes. The question was allowed. It was answered. And the man who asked it was given the highest praise Jesus ever gave anyone, in the same hour.

---

**Brian** *(calm)* I have to be honest about the other side of it. I am a blind believer, and my eyes have not been opened. The healings in these episodes were signs, and signs point to something. They pointed John to who Jesus was. They point me there too, without my sight coming back. I would be lying if I said I never asked for it. I would also be lying if I said that is what this verse is about. This verse is not a promise that every blind person who believes will be healed in this life. It is Jesus answering a question about Himself. John asked, are You the One. And Jesus said, look at what is happening around Me, and remember what Isaiah said would happen when God came. The healings were the proof of who He is. That proof still stands, whether or not my own eyes are ever opened on this side of heaven. I do not need my sight back to know who He is. I know who He is.

**Brian** *(calm)* Blessed is the one who does not fall away on account of me. That is the last line of the answer, and I think it was aimed at John, in prison, who was not going to be healed or rescued and knew it. Jesus was telling him: do not let what I am not doing for you make you doubt what I am. Every blind believer who has prayed for sight and gotten up the next morning still blind has heard that line.

---

**Narrator** *(calm)* Look back over the six episodes. A man born blind sent to wash. Bartimaeus shouting by the road. A man at Bethsaida who saw trees walking and said so. Two men who followed by sound into a house, and one who could not speak for himself. Crowds on a mountain and in the Temple. And now this: when the greatest prophet asked who Jesus was, Jesus pointed at all of them. The blind receive sight. Not because blindness was the worst thing, and not because sight was the point, but because Isaiah had said that when God came, this is what it would look like. Blind people were not left at the edges. They were at the top of the list.

---

**Brian** *(calm)* The part of this episode that comes closest to home for me is not about eyes at all. It is in the verse from Isaiah that Jesus read at Nazareth. He has sent Me to bind up the brokenhearted. The work that everyone in our house does puts us straight in the path of the brokenhearted. It is less a job than a way of life, and it is far harder than most people can imagine. But I am grateful for what it has done to my heart. It keeps me humble. It teaches me to honor other people the way God wants us to, because every one of us is made in His image.

**Brian** *(calm)* I know that someday I will see again. I have always known it, because I was blessed to have parents who led me to the Lord when I was very young.

**Brian** *(calm)* They say blindness is the second greatest fear people have. I take no comfort in that. But I can tell you, from inside it, that blindness is not something to fear at all. What scares me is something else. It is the people who have to carry this burden without knowing the grace and mercy of our Savior, and without knowing the great joys that lie ahead for those of us who belong to Him.
