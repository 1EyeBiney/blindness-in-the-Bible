[BRIAN] {id=019} (calm) Samson lost his sight in the prime of his life. So do a lot of blind adults, and so did I. Most of the people in this series went blind slowly, with age. Samson went blind in a moment, at the height of his strength, and that puts him closer to me and to the people I work with than any of the others. What I think about first is the adjustment. He was the most physically gifted man in the Bible, and in one afternoon he was reduced to being led around by a hand, with his strength gone and his eyes gone with it. The transition was sudden. I had nine months between the diagnosis and the end of my sight, and that was hard. I work all the time with people who lose their vision in a matter of days or hours, and they are the hardest for me to work with, because they had no time to prepare. No time to get their last looks at the things they wanted to remember. Samson got none. The last thing he saw was Delilah's room, and the Philistines in it. The Hebrew word matters to me here in a way it may not to a sighted reader. Judges says the Philistines gouged out his eyes, and the verb is naqar, which the old dictionary defines as to bore, to penetrate, to quarry. Isaiah uses the same word for the quarry a stone is dug from. I have had both of my eyes enucleated, removed, because of the extreme pressure in them before the last surgeries. My doctor described what he was going to do as scooping out the inside of a melon. That is naqar. But I want to say plainly that it was one of my better decisions in life. My eyes had stopped working completely and the pain and discomfort went with them. Samson had no say in it and no anaesthetic, and I know more than I would like about what was done to him. Then there is purpose, and this is the part of Samson's story that is most mine. Between Delilah's room and the hall at Gaza there is a mill, and the text does not say how long he walked in that circle. I know how long mine was. Three years. I had been an IT manager for sixteen years when my sight went, and when it went I did not know what I was for anymore. I spent those three years waiting for God to show me what His purpose was for this stage of my life. I did not get an epiphany. There was no great revelation. What I got was God patiently waiting for me to reach the place where His purpose could be fulfilled in me. While I waited I did not sit still. I went through blind rehab. I earned a Salesforce administrator certification. I kept my computer skills sharp, because I suspected that the life I had already lived would be useful going forward, even if I could not see how.
