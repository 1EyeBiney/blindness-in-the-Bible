# The Law: ideas for a four-part series

Written 13 October 2026, after the six episodes on sight given by Jesus were finished. Nothing here is a script.
These are the options we talked through, kept so Brian can read them and decide when he comes back to it.

## Brian's brief

Four episodes on the Law, in the same shape as the story episodes. No imagined scenes. Something new in their
place: a segment, a new voice that carries information or the plot, or something else. Maps undecided. The
series may produce new sections for the four Law pages on the site.

Before the series, Brian wants to go through the Law pages and others and add his own thoughts, because he is
noticing holes that only he can fill.

## The spine

The Law section's front page already says it: a law is evidence of a problem, because nobody forbids what no
one is doing. Every episode asks what this law shows about how blind people were actually treated.

## The four episodes, in page order

1. **The stumbling block** (Leviticus 19:9-18, Deuteronomy 27:11-19). The law that protects the blind by name.
   The strongest opener: the one law in the ancient world written for us rather than about us.
2. **The servant's eye** (Exodus 21:20-27). The law that sets a blinded servant free. The hardest material, and
   the best for comparison with the other codes.
3. **The blind priest** (Leviticus 21:16-24). The one law that limits a blind person. Needs Brian's voice most.
   His question about touching holy things is already on the page.
4. **The blind animal** (Leviticus 22:17-25, Deuteronomy 15:19-23, Malachi 1:6-14). The law that is not about
   blind people at all. Malachi lets the series end a thousand years after Sinai.

Alternative: put the priest last, so the series ends on the hardest question and Brian's fullest answer.
Claude leans toward page order, because Malachi gives a natural look back across the whole Law.

## Decided 14 October: "The word" replaces the imagined scenes

Each Law page now has a section called The word: the Hebrew word the law turns on, Strong's gloss, where else the
Bible uses it, and what that does to the law (src/words.py). Brian chose this as the segment that stands in for the
imagined scenes. In the episodes it can be its own voice or the narrator's; the pronunciations are on the pages
(mikshol, mik-SHOLE; ayin; chophshi, khof-SHEE; mum, moom; avveret; yare, yah-RAY) and must be tested in the
voices before rendering. The other options below are still open as additions.

## The other options that were on the table

**A. A second voice: the comparison.** A new voice reads the other law codes of the region, word for word from
the translations on the shelf: Hammurabi on the eye, Ur-Nammu, the Egyptian Instruction of Amenemope. The
narrator sets each beside the Bible's law. Real documents instead of invention. Fits episodes 1 and 2, where
Brian's own question about Hammurabi is already on the page.

**B. A second voice: the commentator.** The old writers speak for themselves in a distinct voice: Matthew Henry
in 1706, Josephus in the first century, the 1915 encyclopedia. The narrator introduces each by name and date.
Keeps the named-source discipline audible, not just on the page.

**C. The courtroom.** Each episode poses a case and the narrator works it through the law. A man moves a stone
and a blind neighbour falls: who saw it, who testifies, what court hears it? The stumbling block page already
notices the answer: no witness would come forward, so the law appeals to the fear of God. Reasoning, not
fiction, so it stays inside Brian's rule. Suits episodes 1 and 2.

**D. From the shoes, moved up front.** Brian's experience as the replacement for the scene: a short passage early
in each episode on what the law would mean to him today. Someone moving a thing in his path. Touching
something he was not meant to.

**E. A question put to a pastor.** Pastor John's voice memo has been waiting for a place. A series on the Law is
where a pastor's reading belongs, especially on the priest. Depends on him; a bonus, not the plan.

**Claude's recommendation:** A and B together as one new voice, kept distinct from the Scripture reader
(working name: the reader of the old books). C for the first two episodes. D in all four. Each episode then has
three kinds of voice besides Brian's: Scripture, the old documents, and the narrator reasoning.

## Maps

The Law has no journey. Use the map once, in episode 1, for the two mountains of Deuteronomy 27, Gerizim and
Ebal, where the tribes stood and answered Amen. After that, drop it. Keeping it in every episode would be habit,
not help. (Shechem would need adding to audio/maps/places.json.)

## Sound

No scenes, so the beds change character. The camp at Sinai under episode 1 (the Law banner on the site already
shows it). A household or workshop for the servant. The sanctuary court, with the altar fire, for the priest and
the animals. Brian's music does the rest.

## New sections the series could give the Law pages

- **The other codes, side by side.** A short table on each page: what Hammurabi, Ur-Nammu, Amenemope and the
  Bible each say on that point. Episode 1's page already has the material scattered through its sources.
- **Brian's answer.** The priest page and the stumbling block page carry his questions and no reply from him
  yet. The series would make him write them.
- **What it meant in practice.** Josephus and Henry describe how the laws were applied centuries later. A section
  on each page could say so, drawn from what the episodes gather.

## Decisions for Brian

- The order: page order, or the priest last.
- Which of the five options, and whether the old books get their own voice (and which ElevenLabs voice).
- Whether to ask Pastor John for a reading on the priest.
- Episode length. The Law material is thinner than the stories. Claude suggests 12 to 15 minutes, not 20.
