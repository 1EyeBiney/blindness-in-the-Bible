[NARRATOR] {id=009} (calm) Eli’s own two sons were another matter. Hophni and Phinehas were priests under him, and they were wicked men. They took what was not theirs from the sacrifices and they did worse than that at the door of the tent. Eli heard about it, the text says so. He was old by then, and he spoke to them, and he asked them the question any father would, why do you do these things, and they did not listen. A man of God came and told him that both of them would die on the same day. And hear the rest of what that man said, because it is about Eli himself. Whoever in your house I do not cut off, the LORD said, I will cause your eyes to fail and your heart to grieve. Your eyes. Your heart. Eli was the one not cut off. He lived to ninety-eight. So from that day he knew two things were coming to him, not only to his sons, and keep that in mind when we reach the gate. Eli did not stop his sons. That is the charge against him, and the Bible does not soften it. But hold it next to the other things. He was a father who could not govern his sons and a priest who could govern Israel for forty years. He was unkind to a stranger for one sentence and kind to her for the rest of her life. He failed with the sons he was given and he succeeded with the son he was lent. And when the boy he raised brought him the hardest word of his life, in the chapter we are about to read, his whole answer was this: He is the LORD. Let Him do what is good in His eyes. Samuel is a boy in this chapter. Josephus says he was twelve. And the word of the LORD, the text says, was rare in those days. Where this is. Stand at Shiloh, in the hill country of Ephraim, and face north, with north at twelve o'clock. Shiloh is the first place in this series where we will stand twice, because the next episode happens here too, about a hundred and seventy years later. Bethel is about half a day's walk behind you at seven o'clock. The hill of Jerusalem, which is still a Jebusite town and not yet David's, is a day's walk at six o'clock. And at nine o'clock, a day's walk west toward the coast, is Aphek, where the Philistine army is camped, and across from it a place called Ebenezer, where Israel's army is. Keep nine o'clock in mind. That is the direction the runner comes from.
