Not By Sight, Episode 12: The Voice and the Hands (Genesis 27, Hebrews 11)

Isaac, old and blind, waits in his tent at Beersheba for the son he means to bless. The wrong son walks in. The episode opens with that moment, and with what a blind man hears in it: a familiar voice in the wrong place. Then the chapter itself, read word for word: Rebekah's plan, aimed at every sense her husband had left; the touch, the smell, the question asked twice; the second arrival at the door, when a father who had just picked his son's voice out through a disguise could not tell who was standing there. Then the old books: William Hanks Levy, a blind man writing in London in 1872, who read the chapter and saw a careful blind man; and Matthew Henry, who stopped at the one sense nobody could counterfeit. The Hebrew word for eyes that grow dim. And the end of the story: a second blessing, given on purpose, that Hebrews calls faith. Brian, blind since 2014, on the two kinds of trust a blind man lives by, and on why his own hands made him change his mind about Isaac's.

This is episode 12 of Not By Sight, a study of blindness in the Bible made by a blind man, for listening. It is the second of six on sight lost and not given back: six people in the Bible who lost their sight and lived the rest of their lives without it. Every episode begins with sounds. When a scene is imagined, we say so. Everything else is from the text, from the old books read in their own voices, and from Brian. Scripture is read word for word from the Berean Standard Bible.
