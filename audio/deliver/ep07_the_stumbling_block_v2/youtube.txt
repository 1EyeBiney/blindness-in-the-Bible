Not By Sight, Episode 7: The Stumbling Block (Leviticus 19 and Deuteronomy 27)

One sentence of law, given at Sinai: you must not curse the deaf or place a stumbling block before the blind. Brian, who has been blind since 2014, read it and stopped at one word. For him a stumbling block is an annoyance, something you trip over and catch yourself. So is the law about a trip, or a fall? The answer is in the Hebrew word itself, and in every other place the Bible uses it.

This is episode 7 of Not By Sight, a study of blindness in the Bible made by a blind man, for listening. It is the first of four episodes on the Law: what God commanded about the blind. There are no imagined scenes in this series. Everything you hear is from the text, from the old books read in their own voices, from the Hebrew words, and from Brian. Scripture is read word for word from the Berean Standard Bible.

The map, in words
Stand in Jerusalem and face north, with north at twelve o'clock. Shechem is at twelve o'clock, about a day and a half's walk, between Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, where the tribes stood to answer the curses. Mount Sinai, where the Law was given, lies far to the south, about eleven days' walk, off the bottom edge of the map.
