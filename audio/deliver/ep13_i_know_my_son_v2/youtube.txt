Not By Sight, Episode 13: I know, my son (Genesis 48, Hebrews 11)

Jacob, who once stood in goatskin before his blind father, is a hundred and thirty years old and standing outside his own tent near Hebron when the wagons come up the road from Egypt. The episode opens there, with what a blind man hears in it: wagons, grain, and a way to food in a famine, and the son he had believed dead alive and sending for him. Then seventeen years on, a bed in Goshen, and Genesis 48 read word for word: a dying man who adopts two grown grandsons as his own, who cannot see who is in the room and asks, and who then crosses his hands on purpose. Joseph takes his father's hand to move it. The answer is two words. Then the old books: Matthew Henry on the eye of faith. The Hebrew word for eyes grown heavy, the same word used of Pharaoh's heart. The twelve sons at the bed, the long procession to the cave at Hebron, and the one line about Jacob that Hebrews keeps. Brian, blind since 2014, on provision, on being asked who is there, on how a blind man knows a thing, and on what it costs to say I know when a hand reaches in to move yours.

This is episode 13 of Not By Sight, a study of blindness in the Bible made by a blind man, for listening. It is the third of six on sight lost and not given back: six people in the Bible who lost their sight and lived the rest of their lives without it. Every episode begins with sounds. When a scene is imagined, we say so. Everything else is from the text, from the old books read in their own voice, and from Brian. Scripture is read word for word from the Berean Standard Bible.
