Not By Sight, Episode 16: Between the pillars (Judges 16)

A hall in Gaza on a feast day, three thousand people on the roof alone, and a blind man led in by a boy asks to be put where he can feel the pillars. The episode opens there, with Brian on the most ordinary sentence in the chapter: put my hand on it. Then the whole of Samson's story, told fuller than before: the angel at Zorah, the woman at Timnah who was pleasing to his eyes, the foxes, the gate carried toward Hebron, the Valley of Sorek, and the one person in the country who was paid to hear his secret. Judges 16 read word for word, with one verse for what was done to him and no more. An imagined prison, the mill, and the thing the Philistines did not think to check. Brian, who lost his sight in the prime of his life, on the three years he walked his own circle before he knew what he was for. Matthew Henry on a man who was blind before his eyes were taken; Josephus on a soldier. The last word in the chain, naqar, a quarryman's word, and the bronze that ties the first man of this series to the last. Hebrews 11, where Samson stands with Isaac and Jacob among the people who lived by faith. And at the end of the six, the first verse this program ever read, John 9:3, and what the series found the Bible says about blindness: not whose fault, but whose glory.

This is episode 16 of Not By Sight, a study of blindness in the Bible made by a blind man, for listening. It is the last of six on sight lost and not given back: six people in the Bible who lost their sight and lived the rest of their lives without it. Every episode begins with sounds. When a scene is imagined, we say so. Everything else is from the text, from the old books read in their own voice, and from Brian. Scripture is read word for word from the Berean Standard Bible.

The pictures, in words
The great hall from the end of the last episode, then closer: a small boy and a tall shaven man seen from behind between two massive pillars, a chain at his ankles. A big hand laid flat on the fluted stone. Brian's picture. A title card over the coast at Gaza. The bronze shackles from episode 11. A hill village above the plain and the sea. A woman alone in a stubble field, and far across it a figure of light. A young man with braided hair walking down through vineyards to a town. Fields of grain at dusk with lines of fire running through them. A lone figure far up a moonlit hill with a city gate on his shoulders. A green valley with one house and a lamp in its door. A lamp-lit room: a man asleep with his head on a seated woman's lap, a dark shape waiting in the doorway. The episode map: Gaza in gold, the road up to Zorah. A shaven man from behind pushing the beam of a millstone. A circle worn deep in a dirt floor with a chain lying in it. One high window with light and dust pouring through. A man's head and shoulders from behind, his hair grown long again, hands on the beam. A roof crowded with people looking down into the lit hall. A man between two pillars, one hand on each, his head bowed. Men carrying a shrouded bier up a road to a village on a ridge. Matthew Henry's study, and Josephus in Rome. A pale quarry with blocks half cut from the rock. A wall with six niches and six unlit lamps. Isaac's tent, Jacob's crossed hands, Ahijah in his dark house, Jerusalem besieged at night, and the temple court from episode 1. A closing card over the road up into the hills at evening.
