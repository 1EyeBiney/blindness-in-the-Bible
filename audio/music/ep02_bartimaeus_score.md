# Not By Sight, Episode 2, "Bartimaeus at Jericho": ambient score beds

Written in the same form as the Tentmakers score note so the Suno prompts stay in one family. The rule for the
whole series: same instruments as episode 1 (oud, lyre, reed, frame drum, low drone), no vocals, no choir, no
modern drums, no cinematic bombast, background for speech. What changes here is the room. Episode 1 was Jerusalem,
stone and shadow, a pool at the bottom of a city. Episode 2 is outdoors, in the open air of a hot valley town on a
spring morning, with a road full of people. So the beds lose the chamber and the reverb and gain air, distance,
and a walking pulse.

The section numbers are from `audio/scripts/ep02_bartimaeus_02_sections.md`, and each section is its own file in
`audio/selected/ep02_bartimaeus_v2/section_1/`.

## 1. The "Roadside Dust" bed

Use under sections 001 to 003 (cold open, series intro, where we are), and again under 031 and 032 (the last word
and the close), so the episode opens and closes in the same air.

The vibe: early morning at the edge of a valley town, heat already rising off the ground, a man sitting in the
dust where he sits every day. Open, warm, dry, unhurried. The sound of a place with a lot of sky.

Style/Genre: Ancient Near Eastern open-air ambient, roadside morning underscore.

Prompt: Ancient Near Eastern open-air ambient, dry desert valley morning, soft oud, sparse lyre, warm reed drone,
very light frame drum like a slow walk, wide open sky, no room reverb, dusty roadside outside a palm town, heat
rising, unhurried and grounded, no vocals, no choir, no modern drums, no cinematic bombast, background score for
spoken narration.

## 2. The "Scripture Reading" bed, or none

Use under the READER sections 004, 006, 022, 024, 028, or let them stand bare.

In episode 1 you let the Scripture breathe. I would keep that. If you want something under the longer readings
(006 Mark, 024 Luke 19), a single held drone with no pulse, very low, that you can fade out at the last verse. Not
a new piece of music, just the tail of bed 1 with the strings removed.

## 3. The "Pilgrim Road" bed

Use under 008 (the place: Jericho, palms, balsam, tax station, the climb to Jerusalem, the beggars' fixed places).

The vibe: the busiest road in Judea the week before Passover. Mule trains, carts, families walking, priests going
up to serve, traders from Damascus. Not tense. Purposeful. A long line of people going somewhere together.

Style/Genre: Ancient caravan road ambient, walking pulse.

Prompt: Ancient caravan road ambient, steady walking frame drum pulse like many sandals on packed earth, oud and
lyre in a travelling rhythm, reed melody drifting in and out as if from a passing group, warm valley air, palms and
balsam groves, open space with no echo, pilgrims on the road to a feast, purposeful and warm, no vocals, no choir,
no modern drums, background score for spoken narration.

## 4. The "Crowd Stops" bed

Use under the imagined scene, 009 through 017. This is the one bed in the episode that must change shape in the
middle, and it is where your sound effects live too.

The vibe: Bartimaeus hears the road by ear (the squeaking wheel, the hooves, the sandals), then a crowd unlike the
usual one, turned inward around a voice; he shouts twice; and then, at 015, the crowd stops. The music should stop
with it, or drop to almost nothing, and come back warm at 016 when the crowd calls him forward.

Style/Genre: Open-air crowd suspense into sudden stillness, then warm release.

Prompt: Open-air crowd suspense into sudden stillness, ancient desert road, low bowed drone, sparse oud notes,
restrained frame drum like a crowd walking slowly, tension building as a shout goes unanswered, then the pulse
drops away into a held silence, then warm lyre and reed return as the crowd turns kind, dry air with no reverb,
no vocals, no choir, no modern percussion, leave space for shouted dialogue.

Note for the mix: Suno will not time the drop to section 015 for you. Render the bed, then cut it in Reaper at the
line "The crowd stops. I hear it stop," and either hard-stop the music there or let one low note hang. Your road
ambience (sheep, cattle, chickens off to one side, the cart with the squeaking wheel, sandals) should fall away at
the same moment. That silence is the point of the scene, and it is the one place in the episode where a sighted
viewer and a blind listener will have exactly the same experience.

## 5. The "Brian" bed

Use under 019 and 020, 026, 030 (the scaredy-cat intro and Brian's three sections).

The vibe: the same as episode 1's Brian sections, so the listener knows by sound alone that we have stepped out of
the first century for a minute. Whatever you used under Brian in episode 1 belongs here unchanged. If you want a
Jericho flavour, use bed 1 at a lower level rather than a new piece.

## 6. The "Two Stops in One Town" bed

Use under 023 and 025 (the two Jerichos, Zacchaeus, the sycamore, "the Son of Man came to seek and to save the
lost," teaching with Jerusalem in sight) and 029 (Matthew's two men).

The vibe: the thinking part of the episode, where the narration sets the scenes side by side. Reflective, warm,
with a little lift at "seek and save" but no climax. Daylight, not lamplight.

Style/Genre: Desert hope ambient, open-air reflective underscore.

Prompt: Desert hope ambient, open-air reflective underscore, soft oud harmonics, gentle lyre, faint reed melody,
low warm drone, barely-there frame drum, bright valley daylight, a sycamore fig by a road, a man up a tree, a
teacher who stops twice in one town, measured hope with a small lift and no climax, dry air with no reverb, no
vocals, no choir, no modern drums, gentle background score for spoken narration.

## 7. Transitions at the breaks

The script has twelve `[BREAK]` markers, rendered as two seconds of silence. In episode 1 you filled some of
these with music swells. Here I would fill them with the road instead: a few seconds of ambience alone, no music,
so the listener hears where they are each time the narration pauses. Save the music swells for the two moves out of
the scene (after 017) and into the close (before 032).

## Keeping the family together

What made the episode 1 beds feel like one score was that every prompt repeated the same instrument list and the
same closing clause. Keep both exactly. Change only the room words: "intimate tent" and "chamber" become "open
air," "dry air with no reverb," "wide sky," "roadside." Suno follows those words well. If a result comes back with
room reverb, add "close-miked, no hall" to the prompt and try again.

Because Suno has capped your downloads at sixty, your plan to audition everything in the browser and capture only
the keepers through Reaper's loopback is sound. The music is yours under your paid plan, so recording your own
output is simply another way of saving it. Set the loopback capture to the same 44.1 kHz, 24-bit as your project.
Name the captured files with the bed number and the Suno title, so the credits line and this sheet can be matched
later.
