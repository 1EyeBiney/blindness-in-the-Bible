{{Short description|Roman period street in Jerusalem}}
[[File:Elef Millim - City of David www.mapah.co.il hirodion up.jpg|thumb|right|The street ascending toward the Temple Mount]]

The '''stepped street''', as it is known from academic works,<ref name=ESI2016>{{cite web |first1= Nahshon |last1= Szanton |first2= Joe |last2= Uziel |title= Jerusalem, City of David [stepped street dig, July 2013 - end 2014], Preliminary Report (21/08/2016) |website=[[Hadashot Arkheologiyot]] |publisher=[[Israel Antiquities Authority]] |volume= 128 |year= 2016 |url= http://www.hadashot-esi.org.il/report_detail_eng.aspx?id=25046&mag_id=124 |access-date= 24 July 2020}}</ref> or the '''Jerusalem pilgrim road''' as it has been dubbed by the [[Ir David Foundation]],<ref>[https://www.cityofdavid.org.il/en/events/pilgrimage-road-revealed-%E2%80%93-live-tour The Pilgrimage Road Revealed], City of David homepage, accessed 24 July 2020</ref> is the [[Judaea (Roman province)|early Roman period]] street connecting the [[Temple Mount]] from its southwestern corner, to [[Jerusalem]]'s southern gates of the time via the [[Pool of Siloam]].<ref name=ESI2016/> The stepped street was built at the earliest during the 30s CE, with the latest coin found under the pavement dating to 30–31 CE, during the governorship of [[Pontius Pilate]]<ref name=ESI2016/> of [[New Testament]] fame. The street is part of the [[City of David (archaeological site)|City of David archaeological park]] located in the [[Jerusalem Walls National Park]] in [[East Jerusalem]].  While the area has been referred to as Mount Ophel<ref>{{Cite news |last=Tribune |first=International Herald |date=2003-12-31 |title=Opinion {{!}} 1928: City of David Uncovered: IN OUR PAGES: 100, 75 AND 50 YEARS AGO |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2003/12/31/opinion/IHT-1928-city-of-david-uncovered-in-our-pages-100-75-and-50-years-ago.html |access-date=2026-03-12 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> or the City of David<ref>{{Cite news |last=Feron |first=James |date=1970-08-02 |title=Walking the Walls Of Old Jerusalem |url=https://www.nytimes.com/1970/08/02/archives/walking-the-walls-of-old-jerusalem.html |access-date=2026-03-12 |work=The New York Times |language=en-US |issn=0362-4331}}</ref> by archaeologists and tourists, it is commonly referred to as the [[Wadi Hilweh]] area of [[Silwan]] by local residents.

==Description==
{{See also|Jerusalem Water Channel}}

[[File:City of David - The Stepped Pilgrims Road IMG 5939.JPG|thumb|Idealized representation of the route from the [[Pool of Siloam]] to the [[Temple in Jerusalem|Temple Mount]]]]

The street ascended from Jerusalem's southern gates,<ref name=ESI2016/> along the ancient [[City of David (historic)|City of David]], today part of the [[Palestinians|Palestinian]] neighborhood of [[Silwan]], into what is now the [[Old City (Jerusalem)|Old City]] and passed by the [[Western Wall]]<ref name=Ackerman>{{cite news |author= Ackerman, Gwen |date= 25 January 2011 |url= https://www.bloomberg.com/news/2011-01-25/archaeologists-discover-ancient-pilgrim-road-through-jerusalem-s-old-city.html |title= Archaeologists Discover Ancient Pilgrim Road Through Jerusalem's Old City |publisher= Bloomberg |access-date=18 January 2014}}</ref> after passing underneath the Herodian bridge now known as [[Robinson's Arch]].<ref name=ESI2016/> The ancient path was improved and paved in large, well-cut stone in the pattern of two steps followed by a long landing, followed by two more steps and another landing. The street was eight meters wide and its length from the Pool of Siloam to the Temple Mount is 600 meters.<ref name=Hasson>{{cite news |date=14 September 2009 |title= Archeologists find main Jerusalem street from Second Temple period |author= Hasson, Nir |url= https://www.haaretz.com/print-edition/news/archeologists-find-main-j-lem-street-from-second-temple-period-1.7951 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20110606215015/http://www.haaretz.com/print-edition/news/archeologists-find-main-j-lem-street-from-second-temple-period-1.7951 |url-status= dead |archive-date= June 6, 2011 |newspaper= Haaretz |access-date=18 January 2014}}</ref>

The [[Jerusalem Water Channel|large drainage channel]] which ran below the street<ref name=ESI2016/> has also been excavated and made partially accessible to visitors.

==Archaeological exploration history==
Sections of the ancient street were first discovered by [[Charles Warren]] in 1884,<ref name=ESI2016/> followed by [[Frederick J. Bliss]] and [[Archibald C. Dickey]] of the [[Palestine Exploration Fund]] between 1894 and 1897. The find was reburied when their excavation concluded. Other sections were uncovered, then reburied, by later archaeologists, Jones in 1937 and [[Kathleen Kenyon]] in  1961–1967.<ref>[http://www.gojerusalem.com/article_1176/Second-Temple-Period-Street-Discovered Second Temple Period Street Discovered, at gojerusalem.com] {{webarchive |url= https://web.archive.org/web/20100617084411/http://www.gojerusalem.com/article_1176/Second-Temple-Period-Street-Discovered |date= 17 June 2010}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |author= Bedien, David |title= Ancient Temple Street Discovered Beneath Jerusalem |date= 20 September 2009 |newspaper= Philadelphia Bulletin |url= http://thebulletin.us/articles/2009/09/20/news/world/doc4ab6793d7be0b285938366.txt |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20160303235027/http://thebulletin.us/articles/2009/09/20/news/world/doc4ab6793d7be0b285938366.txt |url-status= dead |archive-date= 3 March 2016}} <!-- thebulletin.us belongs to a Columbus, GA, newspaper now--></ref> More details about the street were published by [[Benjamin Mazar]] in 1969–1971, followed by [[Ronny Reich]] (alone or in collaboration with Yakov Billig, then [[Eli Shukron]]) between 1999 and 2011 (including findings from the drainage channel running beneath the street), and [[Eilat Mazar]] in 2000.<ref name=ESI2016/>

The street was re-excavated during excavations on a [[Jerusalem Water Channel|water channel]] from the [[Second Temple period]].<ref name=Ackerman/>

== Reception ==
The [[Israel Antiquities Authority]] performed the excavation beneath existing homes using a technique called horizontal excavation.  According to American archaeologist [[Jodi Magness]], “Methodologically, the problem of using a horizontal or tunnel excavation in archaeology is that it doesn’t allow you to see the full context of the remains. Archaeology is all about context, but by digging in this way, you divorce the remains that you excavate from the remains that are above them and around them and that is problematic from an archaeological point of view.”  When asked about the criticism, archaeologist Ari Levy said technology has allowed the archaeologists to overcome many of the obstacles of the excavation, noting that they can't just remove the neighbors’ houses.<ref>{{Cite news |last=Borschel-Dan |first=Amanda |date=17 October 2019 |title=On an ancient road to the Temple, archaeological innovation, mystery and dispute |url=https://www.timesofisrael.com/on-an-ancient-road-to-the-temple-archaeological-innovation-mystery-and-dispute/ |access-date=12 October 2024 |work=Times of Israel}}</ref>

The Ir David Foundation, also known as Elad, is a [[Israeli settlement|settler organisation]] that leads excavations in the locale.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news |date=2019-06-30 |title=U.S. Envoy Swings Sledgehammer in East Jerusalem, and a Metaphor Is Born (Published 2019) |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2019/06/30/world/middleeast/ambassador-friedman-jerusalem.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20251221095924/https://www.nytimes.com/2019/06/30/world/middleeast/ambassador-friedman-jerusalem.html |archive-date=2025-12-21 |access-date=2026-03-14 |language=en}}</ref> Archaeologists from the [[Israel Antiquities Authority]] spent twenty years excavating the site.<ref name="Tercatin">{{Cite news |last=Tercatin |first=Rossella |date=2026-02-02 |title=2,000-year-old Pilgrimage Road to Temple Mount opens to public after years of digging |url=https://www.timesofisrael.com/2000-year-old-pilgrimage-road-to-temple-mount-opens-to-public-after-years-of-digging/ |access-date=2026-03-14 |work=The Times of Israel |language=en-US |issn=0040-7909}}</ref>  The non-governmental organisation [[Emek Shaveh]] has characterised the archaeological work initiated by Elad an "attempt to redefine the area solely as an ancient site, and not as a Palestinian residential neighborhood".<ref>{{Cite web |date=2015-10-28 |title=Underground Jerusalem: The excavation of tunnels, channels, and underground spaces in the Historic Basin |url=https://emekshaveh.org/en/underground-jerusalem-the-excavation-of-tunnels-channels-and-underground-spaces-in-the-historic-basin/ |access-date=2026-03-14 |website=Emek Shaveh |language=en}}</ref> In 2019 Elad opened a portion of the road with [[David M. Friedman]], the [[United States Ambassador to Israel]], giving a keynote. According to David M. Halbfinger in ''New York Times'' the ambassador's presence at a public event in East Jerusalem was unusual as ambassadors typically try to "avoid being seen as taking Israel's side in the conflict with the Palestinians".<ref name=":0" /><ref name="Tercatin"/>

[[United States Secretary of State]] [[Marco Rubio]] and United States ambassador to Israel [[Mike Huckabee]] were both present at the inauguration of the site in 2025.  Rubio's visit was criticized by Palestinians and their supporters as endorsing Israeli control and actions in the area.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-09-16 |title=Marco Rubio inaugurates Israeli settler-led archaeological site |url=https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cg42v2r10kyo |access-date=2026-03-12 |website=www.bbc.com |language=en-GB}}</ref> Ze’ev Orenstein, director of international affairs at the City of David, stated that “All the archeological excavations are carried out by the Israel Antiquities Authority according to the highest standards.”<ref>{{Cite news |last=Lewis |first=Simon |date=2025-09-16 |title=Rubio visits Jerusalem archeological site in boost to Israel's claims |url=https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/rubio-visits-jerusalem-archeological-site-boost-israels-claims-2025-09-15/ |access-date=2026-03-13 |work=Reuters}}</ref>

==Interpretation==
According to archaeologist Ronny Reich, who for several years led the dig uncovering the ancient street together with archaeologist Eli Shukron, pilgrims used the Pool of Siloam as a [[mikveh]] for ritual purification before walking up the street to the [[Second Temple|Temple]].<ref name=Hasson/> However, Yoel Elitzur opposes this interpretation and argues that the Pool of Siloam was a typical Roman public swimming pool.<ref name=Elitzur>{{cite journal |last= Elitzur |first= Yoel |title= The Siloam Pool — 'Solomon's Pool' — Was a Swimming Pool |journal= Palestine Exploration Quarterly |year= 2008 |volume= 140 |issue= 1 (2008) |pages= 17–25 |doi= 10.1179/003103208x269114 |s2cid= 161774603 |url= https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233496309 |access-date=21 July 2020}}</ref>

In ancient times,{{When |date= August 2024}} in the celebration called [[Simchat Beit HaShoeivah]], water was carried up from the [[Pool of Siloam]] to the [[Temple in Jerusalem|Temple]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Mishnah Sukkah 4:10 |website= sefaria.org |url=https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Sukkah.4.10?lang=bi&with=all&lang2=en}} For Mishnah Sukkah article see [[Moed]].</ref>{{dubious |Who says that this particular street built in 30 CE is ''the'' one used in the festival? When (in what historical period) was this festival celebrated? Who connects the two? Source? Primary sources not accepted. |date= August 2024}} There are attempts to connect the Roman-period street to this festival.
