[[File:Huldah Gates3344.JPG|thumb|Reconstruction of the Herodian Hulda Gates.]]
The '''Huldah Gates''' ({{langx|he|שערי חולדה}}, ''Sha'arei Hulda'') were one of the [[Gates of the Old City of Jerusalem]] leading into the [[Jerusalem Temple]] compound in the [[Hasmonean]] period and were named as such in the [[Mishnah]].<ref name="EJ963"/> The term is currently being used for the remains of two later sets of gates, the Triple Gate and [[the Double Gate]], known together as the Huldah Gates,<ref>{{cite book |last= Gonen |first= Rivka |title= Contested Holiness: Jewish, Muslim, and Christian Perspectives on the Temple Mount in Jerusalem |publisher=[[Ktav Publishing House]] |date=2003 |location= Jersey City, N.J. |page=[https://archive.org/details/contestedholines00rivk/page/32 32] |language=en |oclc= 470185481 |isbn= 9780881257984 |url= https://archive.org/details/contestedholines00rivk |url-access= registration }}</ref> built as part of the much extended [[Herod the Great|Herodian]] [[Temple Mount]], situated in [[Old City (Jerusalem)|Jerusalem's Old City]].<ref name="OConnor"/> Both sets of gates were set into the [[Southern Wall]] of the Temple compound and gave access to the Temple Mount esplanade by means of underground vaulted ramps.<ref name="OConnor"/> Both were walled up in the Middle Ages.<ref name="OConnor">{{cite book |title= The Holy Land: An Oxford Archaeological Guide from Earliest Times to 1700 |series= Oxford Archaeological Guides |author= Jerome Murphy-O'Connor |year= 2008 |location= Oxford |publisher= Oxford University Press |isbn=978-0-19-923666-4 |url= https://books.google.com/books?id=m3Yy9FDcT8gC&q=Hulda&pg=PA113 |pages= 104, 113 |access-date=27 July 2019 }}</ref>

The western set is a double-arched gate (the '''Double Gate'''), and the eastern is a triple-arched gate (the '''Triple Gate''').<ref name="OConnor"/> There still are a few Herodian architectural elements visible outside and inside the gates, while most everything else of what we see today is later, Muslim-period work.<ref name="OConnor"/>

==Etymology==
The name "Huldah gates" is taken from the description of the Temple Mount in the [[Mishnah]] (Tractate of [[Middot (Talmud)|Midot]] 1:3).<ref name="EJ963">Encyclopædia Judaica (ed. 1972), vol. 15, pp. 963-4</ref>

Two possible etymologies are given for the name: "Huldah" means "mole" or "mouse" in Hebrew, and the tunnels leading up from these gates called to mind the holes or tunnels used by these animals. Alternatively, in a possible folk etymology, the [[First Temple]] prophetess [[Huldah]]<ref>2 Kgs 22:14-20</ref><ref>Encyclopædia Judaica (ed. 1972), vol. 8, p. 1063</ref> was said to have held court in this area and, indeed, her tomb<ref>Encyclopædia Judaica (ed. 1972), vol. 9, p. 1553 (Tos. Neg. 6:2)</ref> was placed here by some as well.

==Identification==
Accepted opinion amongst scholars is that the Mishna's description (see under [[Huldah Gates#Etymology|Etymology]]) refers to the sanctified area of the Temple Mount in the [[Hasmonean]] period. Therefore, calling the gates found in the current southern wall "Huldah" would be an [[anachronism]], as the base of that wall is part of [[Herod the Great|Herod]]'s post-Hasmonean extension of the Temple Mount.{{citation needed|date=July 2019}}

==Archaeology==
The 19th century excavations of the area by [[Charles Warren]]<ref>Encyclopædia Judaica (ed. 1972), vol. 9, p. 1525</ref> discovered an erratic series of passageways under the triple gate, some leading below the wall and beyond the Mount's southern edge. The purpose and age of these passages are unknown, and more recent archaeologists have not been allowed to investigate due to the political volatility of the site. The passageways from both gates are now used by the [[waqf]] as [[mosque]]s.{{citation needed|date=July 2019}}

==Description==
Both the Double and Triple Gates of the Herodian time allowed access to the Temple Mount esplanade via a vestibule followed by underground vaulted ramps.<ref name="OConnor"/> They have both been blocked since the Middle Ages, and the underground access routes have been partially or fully reconstructed during the Umayyad and later reconstructions and repair work.<ref name="OConnor"/>

===Double Gate===
[[The Double Gate]] was blocked by the construction of a medieval tower in front of it, probably during Fatimid era.<ref name="madaindouble">{{cite web |title=Double Huldah Gate (al-Aqsa al-Qadimi) |url=https://madainproject.com/double_hulda_gate |website=Madain project |access-date=1 September 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190901151659/https://madainproject.com/double_hulda_gate |archive-date=1 September 2019 |location=WayBack Machine}}</ref><ref name="OConnor"/> Today a small library is established within the remains of this medieval-era fortification tower.<ref name="madaindouble" /> Of what we see today, which is part of the right (eastern) gate, the lintel and the relieving arch above it are Herodian, while the intricately carved cornice is [[Umayyad]].<ref name="OConnor"/> Only the eastern portal of the twin, now blocked, gate is partially visible today.<ref name="madaindouble" />

Inside the Double Gate there is a vestibule with large columns (at least three) supporting two pairs of domes.<ref name="madaindouble" /> The central columns and two more flanking the steps are Herodian, and have recently been reinforced with concrete frames.<ref name="madaindouble" /> The domes though seem to be from an early seventh-century (Early Muslim) reconstruction.<ref name="OConnor"/>

When the [[Al-Aqsa Mosque]] was built, the old steps were blocked, and the eastern aisle lengthened so that new steps from its end would exit north of the mosque, so essentially the exit on the Temple Mount moved eastwards somewhat. The mosque's [[al-Khataniyya Library]] was also established in the remains of the Fatimid-era fortification tower, today entered through the western portal of the twin gate.<ref name="madaindouble" />

===Triple Gate===
The Triple Gate was blocked in the 11th century.<ref name="OConnor"/> The western [[jamb]] is Herodian, while the rest of what we see today is [[Umayyad]].<ref name="OConnor"/>

Inside the Triple Gate, in Herodian times there must have been a passageway identical to the one inside the Double Gate.<ref name="OConnor"/> Its remains now connect to the so-called "[[Solomon's Stables|Al-Marwani Mosque (Solomon's Stables)]]", initially a pillared Herodian substructure of his enlarged Temple esplanade, with some Herodian elements still visible, but repeatedly repaired and rebuilt over the centuries and used since 1996 as a mosque.<ref name="OConnor"/>

The interior of the Triple Gate is similar to that of the Double Gate, though the longer aisle is to the west, and its third aisle, on the east, forms the western boundary of the vaulted area known as ''[[Solomon's Stables|Al-Marwani Mosque (Solomon's Stables)]]''.{{citation needed|date=July 2019}}
