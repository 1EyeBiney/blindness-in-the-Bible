{{Short description|Ancient Israelite town in the Gilead, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible}}
'''Jabesh-Gilead''' ({{Langx|he|יָבֵשׁ גִּלְעָד}} ''Yāḇēš Gilʿāḏ''), sometimes shortened to '''Jabesh''', was an ancient [[Israelite]] town in [[Gilead]], in northwest [[Jordan]]. Jabesh is mentioned several times in the [[Hebrew Bible]] primarily in connection with [[King Saul]]'s battles against the [[Ammon]]ites and [[Philistines]].

Jabesh Gilead means Jabesh of Gilead or Jabesh in Gilead, i.e. the town Jabesh in the region of Gilead, as opposed to other towns called Jabesh in other parts of the country.

Jabesh means "dry" in [[Hebrew language|Hebrew]],<ref>{{cite web|title=morfix dictionary|url=http://www.morfix.co.il/%D7%99%D7%91%D7%A9}}</ref> a name possibly attributed to the site's well-draining soil.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last=McDonald|first=Burton|url=https://www.bu.edu/asor/pubs/books-monographs/macdonald.pdf|title=East of the Jordan: Territories and Sites of the Hebrew Scriptures|publisher=American Schools of Oriental Research|year=2000|pages=202–203}}</ref> Gilead means 'heap [of stones] of testimony'. There is also an alternative theory that it means 'rocky region'. [Smith's Bible Dictionary, "Gil'e-ad"].

==History==

=== In the Hebrew Bible ===
Jabesh-Gilead is mentioned in the [[Book of Judges]],<ref>{{cite web|title=Judges 21:8-15 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Judges%2021:%208-15&version=9;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref> 
in the first<ref>{{cite web|title=1 Samuel 11:1-11 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2011:%201-11;&version=49;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=1 Samuel 31:11-13 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Samuel%2031:%2011-13&version=9;;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref> and second<ref>{{cite web|title=2 Samuel 2:4-5 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Samuel%202:%204,5&version=9;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=2 Samuel 21:12 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Samuel%2021:%2012&version=9;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref> [[Books of Samuel]], and in the [[Book of Chronicles]].<ref>{{cite web|title=1 Chronicles 10:11-12 (Bible)|url=http://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Chronicles%2010:%2011,12&version=9;|publisher=Bible Gateway|access-date=2007-12-08}}</ref>

==== Benjaminite War ====

Jabesh is mentioned in the biblical episode of the [[Levite's concubine]], also known as the Benjamite War,{{Citation needed|date=July 2026}} during which eleven tribes of Israel had massacred the [[Tribe of Benjamin]]. The eleven tribes relented from wiping out the whole tribe, and decided that they needed to find wives for the 600 remaining Benjaminite men since all other people in Benjamin had been killed. However, they had taken an oath not to give their daughters to a Benjaminite, so they found the one city in Israel that had not joined the fight: Jabesh. The city's inhabitants were executed under the [[Herem (war or property)|Herem]] except for 400 virgins. They brought back 400 virgins from Jabesh and gave them to the men on Rimmon Rock ({{Bibleverse|Judges|21:8-15}}).[[File:067.The_Benjaminites_Take_the_Virgins_of_Jabesh-gilead.jpg|thumb|The Benjaminites take the virgins of Jabesh-gilead, drawing by [[Gustave Doré]]]]

==== Nahash of Ammon ====
Jabesh-Gilead is a central setting of {{Bibleverse|1 Samuel|11}}. After [[Saul]] is [[Anointing|anointed]] by [[Samuel]], [[Nahash of Ammon]] attacks Jabesh-Gilead. Having subjected the town to a [[siege]], its inhabitants sought terms for [[Surrender (military)|surrender]], but were told by Nahash that they had a choice of death by sword or having their right eyes gouged out. The population obtained seven days' grace from Nahash, during which they would be allowed to seek help from the Israelites, after which they would have to submit to the terms of surrender. The town's inhabitants sought help from the people of Israel, sending messengers throughout the whole territory, and [[Saul]] responded by raising an army which decisively defeated Nahash and his cohorts at [[Adoni-Bezek#Bezek|Bezek]]. After the war is over, the Israelites assemble at [[Gilgal]] to renew Saul's kingship over [[Kingdom of Israel (united monarchy)|Israel]].

==== Saul's death ====

After Saul [[Suicide methods|falls on his sword]] during the [[Battle of Gilboa]], the victorious Philistines recover his body as well as those of his three sons who also died in the battle, decapitate them and display them on the wall of [[Beit She'an]]. At night, the inhabitants of Jabesh-Gilead retrieve the bodies, showing their devotion to the rescuer of the city. Following a funeral pyre, the bones of Saul and his sons were buried under the [[Tamarix|tamarisk]] tree in Jabesh ({{Bibleverse|1 Samuel|31:8-13|9}}, {{Bibleverse|1 Chronicles|10:12|9}}). When David learns it was the men of Jabesh-Gilead who had brought Saul to an honorable burial, he sent messengers to bless them "And David sent messengers to the men of Yavesh-Gilad, and said to them: ‘May you be blessed of Hashem, that you have shown this kindness to your lord, to Saul, and buried him.’”({{Bibleverse|2 Samuel|2:4-7|9}}). Later, David takes the bones of Saul and of his son Jonathan from Jabesh and buries them in [[Zelah, Judea|Zela]], in the tomb of [[Kish (Bible)|Saul's father]] ({{Bibleverse|2 Samuel|21:12-14|9}}).[[File:077.Jabesh-Gileadites_Recover_the_Bodies_of_Saul_and_His_Sons.jpg|thumb|Jabesh-Gileadites recover the bodies of Saul and his sons, Gustave Doré]]

==== House of Shallum ====
In the [[Books of Kings]] ({{Bibleverse|2 Kings|15:10|9}}, {{Bibleverse|2 Kings|15:13-14|9}}) [[Shallum of Israel]]'s father is identified as [[Jabesh]]. However, the passage may instead mention a [[toponym]], identifying that Shallum was "the son" of a city called Jabesh. In this view, Shallum may have originated from Jabesh-Gilead.<ref name="Freedman2">Freedman, Myers (2000), p. 664</ref>

=== Later years ===
In his ''[[Antiquities of the Jews]]'' (c. 93–94 CE), [[Josephus]] describes Jabesh Gilead as a metropolis in the Transjordan. In the early-4th century CE, Greek scholar [[Eusebius]] mentioned "Iabeis Galaad" in his ''[[Onomasticon (Eusebius)|Onomasticon]]'' as a "village beyond the Jordan located on the mountains six miles from the city of [[Pella, Jordan|Pella]] on the road to [[Jerash|Gerasa]]."<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last=McDonald|first=Burton|url=https://www.bu.edu/asor/pubs/books-monographs/macdonald.pdf|title=East of the Jordan: Territories and Sites of the Hebrew Scriptures|publisher=American Schools of Oriental Research|year=2000|pages=202–203}}</ref>

==Identification==

The exact location of Jabesh-Gilead is debated. Most scholars identify it with [[Tell el-Maqlub]],<ref>{{Cite book|last=Merrill|first=S|title=East of the Jordan: a record of travel and observation in the countries of Moab, Gilead, and Bashan. R. Bentley|year=1883|location=New York: Scribner's|pages=440|orig-date=1881}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal|last=Noth|first=M.|date=1953|title=Jabesh-gilead: Ein Beitrag zur Methode alttestamentischer Topographie|journal=Zeitschrift des Deutschen Palästina-Vereins|pages=28–41}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last=Simons|first=J|title=The Geographical and Topographical Texts of the Old Testament: A Concise Commentary in XXXII Chapters|publisher=Brill|year=1959|location=Leiden|pages=315}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last=Aharoni|first=Y.|title=The Land of the Bible: A Historical Geography|publisher=Burns & Oates|year=1979|editor-last=Rainey|editor-first=A.F.|edition=2|location=London|pages=379, 437}}</ref><ref>{{Cite book|last1=Finkelstein|first1=Israel|title=Ugarit-Forschungen; Band 43 (2011)|last2=Lipschits|first2=Oded|last3=Koch|first3=Ido|year=2012|isbn=978-3-86835-086-9|location=[Erscheinungsort nicht ermittelbar]|pages=131|chapter=The Biblical Gilead: Observations on Identifications, Geographic Divisions and Territorial History.|oclc=1101929531}}</ref> but [[Tell Abu al-Kharaz]] is also frequently suggested.<ref>{{Cite journal|last=Glueck|first=Nelson|date=February 1943|title=Jabesh-Gilead|url=http://dx.doi.org/10.2307/3218934|journal=Bulletin of the American Schools of Oriental Research|volume=89|issue=89 |pages=2–6|doi=10.2307/3218934|jstor=3218934 |s2cid=222428752 |issn=0003-097X|url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref>{{Cite journal|last1=Fischer|first1=Peter M.|last2=Herrmann|first2=Georgina|date=January 1995|title=A Carved Bone Object from Tell Abu al-Kharaz in Jordan: A Palestinian Workshop for Bone and Ivory?|url=http://dx.doi.org/10.1179/lev.1995.27.1.145|journal=Levant|volume=27|issue=1|pages=145–163|doi=10.1179/lev.1995.27.1.145|issn=0075-8914|url-access=subscription}}</ref> Both sites were inhabited during the Iron Age and lie along [[Wadi Yabis|Wadi al-Yabis]], a stream believed to preserve the town's ancient name.<ref name=":0">{{Cite book|last=McDonald|first=Burton|url=https://www.bu.edu/asor/pubs/books-monographs/macdonald.pdf|title=East of the Jordan: Territories and Sites of the Hebrew Scriptures|publisher=American Schools of Oriental Research|year=2000|pages=202–203}}</ref>
